# Search

`GET /v1/search?q=...` finds pages by their title and their text, among the pages the caller may read. Full-text and title search, and, when the semantic search is on and its model is ready, the pages found by meaning fused with them (the last section).

## What it takes and gives

| Parameter | Rule |
|---|---|
| `q` | 1 to 200 characters after trimming. Anything else is 422 `invalid`. It is passed to PostgreSQL as a bound parameter of `websearch_to_tsquery`, never put into a statement. |
| `collectionId` | Only this collection. One the caller may not read gives an empty list with status 200, never 404, so the answer says nothing about collections the caller cannot see. |
| `limit` | 1 to 50, default 20 (narrower than the lists of the API: a headline costs about 4.6 ms a page). |
| `cursor` | What the previous page returned. A cursor this server did not issue is 422 `invalid`. |

A result is `{ pageId, urlId, title, snippet: { text, ranges }, collectionId, rank, updatedAt }`, and `partial: true` when only the start of the page is searchable, and `matchedPassage` when the semantic search found it (below). `semantic` says whether the semantic search answered, and `nextCursor` is `null` on the last page. **There is no total**: it would say how many matching pages the caller cannot see.

The route is limited to 30 requests in 60 seconds for each person (429 after that).

**The snippet is plain text.** `text` is at most 300 characters, and `ranges` are the `[start, end)` offsets, in UTF-16 code units, of the matched words: inside the text and not overlapping. The server never sends markup; the client draws the text as text and wraps the ranges in `mark`. The headline is cut from the page text by `ts_headline` with U+0001 and U+0002 as the match markers. Those two characters are replaced by spaces in the page text before it is handed to `ts_headline`, so every marker in its output is the server's own, and nothing a page contains can fake a match or reach the client as markup. When a page matched by its title only, or its match lies beyond the first 200,000 characters, the snippet is the first 160 characters of the page with no ranges.

## Who sees what

The collections the caller may read are looked up first (`visibleCollections`) and applied **inside the query**, together with the caller's tenant and `deleted_at is null`; ranking, the limit and the cursor only ever see readable pages. The headline statement repeats the same checks, so a page moved out of reach between the two statements is left out rather than read. A person who may read no collection gets an empty list without a query. A search never crosses tenants.

## How it is matched and ranked

- **Text:** `pages.tsv`, maintained by a trigger, holds the title (weight A) and the body (weight B) under the `simple` configuration: no stemming, accents kept, and Chinese and Japanese runs are one token each. `ts_rank_cd` ranks.
- **The tsvector limit.** PostgreSQL refuses a `tsvector` over 1,048,575 bytes. A page whose text does not fit is **never an error on save and never unsearchable**: the trigger retries with the first 600,000, 300,000, 150,000, 70,000, 30,000 and 10,000 characters of the body, and as a last resort the title alone. The page is found by its title and by the words of the indexed prefix, its `search_partial` flag is true, and the result says `partial: true` (the web client shows "Only the beginning of this page is searchable"). A word beyond the indexed prefix is not found. A long text with repeated words fits whole: the limit is about distinct words, not length.
- **Titles that are not typed exactly.** A query of three characters or more also matches a title by trigram similarity (above 0.3, `pg_trgm`) or as a substring, ranked at half the similarity, so a full-text hit always outranks a typo hit. A query of two characters or more that holds a character outside ASCII may also match a title by substring: a run of Chinese or Japanese is one token to the `simple` parser, so substring is the only way to find such a title by one of its words. A short ASCII query matches no title by similarity or substring (it would match nearly all of them). `%` and `_` in the query are characters, not patterns.
- **Order:** rank, then the last edit (newest first), then the page id: a total order, paged by a keyset cursor `base64url({"k":[rank, edit time in microseconds, id]})`. Rank is recomputed on every request, so a page edited between two requests can move; a page is never repeated or skipped by the cursor itself.

## Search by meaning (hybrid)

When `GRIMOIRE_SEMANTIC_SEARCH` is `on` and the model is ready, the same route also asks which chunks of pages are nearest in meaning to the query, and fuses the two lists. `semantic` is then `true` (the semantic search answered; its list may be empty); otherwise the answer is the full-text search above, exactly, with `semantic: false`.

- **The two lists.** The full-text list is the top `GRIMOIRE_SEMANTIC_CANDIDATES` pages (default 100) by the order above. The semantic list is the best chunk of each of the nearest pages, from the 300 nearest chunks the caller may read, at most `GRIMOIRE_SEMANTIC_CANDIDATES` pages. Both are asked for at once. The collections, the tenant and "not in the trash" are applied **inside** the semantic statement, which reads the passage in the same statement, so nothing of a page the caller cannot read is ever a candidate, a count, a passage or a cursor.
- **Fusion.** A page scores the sum, over the lists it is in, of `1 / (60 + rank)` (ranks from 1); the order is the score, then the last edit (newest first), then the page id: a total order. A page in both lists outranks a page in one at the same rank, and the two orders interleave: the nearest page by meaning ties the first page by words. `rank` in an item is that score.
- **A page found only by meaning is limited.** At most **5** of them in one answer (`MEANING_ONLY_MAX` in core), the nearest five, after the fusion and before the page is cut out. Pages found by words, alone or with meaning, are never dropped. The reason is measured (`docs/proof/m7-semantic-quality.md`): the models' similarity for a query that matches nothing lies in the same band as the similarity of real answers (0.73 to 0.79 for nonsense against 0.78 to 0.89 for answers, base model), so no minimum similarity separates them, and a nearest-neighbour search always has nearest pages. A search that matches no word therefore returns at most five pages, each with the passage that was found, so that the screen shows why. `GRIMOIRE_SEMANTIC_MIN_SIMILARITY` stays `0` by default.
- **`matchedPassage`.** An item whose best chunk came from the semantic list has `matchedPassage: { text }`: the plain text of that chunk, white space made one space, control characters removed, cut at a word boundary to at most 300 characters; no markup, no ranges. A page found only by words has none and keeps its highlighted `snippet`; a page found only by meaning has a `snippet` equal to the first 160 characters of the passage with no ranges, so an older client still shows something.
- **The paging.** `cursor` is `base64url({"h":[score, edit time in microseconds, id]})`, under its own key so it cannot be taken for the cursor of the full-text answer (`{"k":...}`, whose first number is a text rank). Each request asks for both lists again and finds its place by the key, so a row is returned once as long as the index is the same between two requests. **A page beyond the 100th candidate of both lists is not reachable** (the candidate limit above). A cursor of the full-text answer continues in the full-text search, always.
- **The fallback.** When the setting is off, the model is `loading` or `unavailable`, the query is not embedded in time (`GRIMOIRE_SEMANTIC_QUERY_TIMEOUT_MS`, default 1,500), or the semantic statement fails, the answer is the full-text search alone (the page that was asked for, with its own cursor), `semantic: false`, and `search.semantic_fallback` (debug, with the reason and never the text of the search) is logged. A failing semantic part never fails the request. **One exception: a request that carries the cursor of a hybrid answer cannot be answered from another order.** If the semantic part is gone by then, it is 503 `unavailable` ("try again"), not a page that skips or repeats rows.
- **The model.** The chunks are read for the model in use: `modelKey(model)` = the model's name and the version of its weights (`multilingual-e5-base-int8@3fa9c0d1`). New weights re-embed every page, and until they have, the search finds nothing by meaning for the pages not yet re-embedded.
- **Not built, to revisit when there is real data:** a **flatness gate**, which would drop the pages found only by meaning when the top of the semantic list is flat (the nearest page is hardly nearer than the fifth), with a new setting off by default. On the 46-passage corpus of the quality record the top of the list is flat for nonsense (the nearest page is 0.007 to 0.031 above the fifth, against a median of 0.066 for real queries), but that shape depends on how many pages there are, so a threshold tuned there would not transfer to a workspace. The explicit cap above is what stands until it is measured on real searches.
- **Not measured yet: the fused list against the full-text list.** The recall of the model alone is recorded in `docs/proof/m7-semantic-quality.md`. Whether the fused list reaches at least the recall of the full-text list alone on the same queries, and that a query for a rare identifier still returns its page first, needs the real model and the database in one run (the exit test of the milestone); until that run there is no figure for the fused list.
- **When to change the index.** The semantic statement is an exact scan with no approximate index: about 40 ms over 20,000 chunks and 1.6 to 12.5 ms when the filter keeps 5 to 50 collections (`docs/research/13-semantic-search.md`), roughly 400 ms unfiltered at 200,000 chunks. **The table must be revisited (an HNSW index with `hnsw.iterative_scan = relaxed_order`, and `halfvec` to halve the storage) when it passes 200,000 chunks (about 14,000 pages) or when the measured 95th percentile of the semantic statement passes 400 ms.** `server.stats` carries the number of chunks so that the operator sees it coming.

## Limits to know

- **Raw HTML blocks are not searched.** The stored search text leaves out the text inside a block of raw HTML (a block is one node and contributes nothing; inline HTML keeps the text between its tags). A word that appears only inside such a block, which is what an imported callout or toggle can be before conversion, is not found. This is the default in force; whether the search text should include the visible text of raw HTML blocks is the owner's decision (`docs/proof/m2-conversion.md`, "Search text").
- **No body trigram search.** A typo in a body word finds nothing; only titles tolerate typos (a trigram index over bodies cost 164 MB and twice the query time in the measurements).
- **Statement timeout.** One search may run `GRIMOIRE_SEARCH_STATEMENT_TIMEOUT_MS` (default 3,000). Past it the statement is cancelled, the route answers 503 `unavailable` and logs `search.timeout`.
- **Nothing of a query is logged.** `search.query` (debug) carries the milliseconds and the number of results only.
